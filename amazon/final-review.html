Presentation cue card · 9:00 target

How do you fit a big AI model on a small computer? Quantization explained

Hook and intro 0:00 to 1:00

In the next nine minutes I want to show you how to make a big model fit on a small computer, and how to know if it still works.

Ask: "Has anyone downloaded an AI model, pressed run, and got out of memory?" Pause. Look around. Picture it: the model is a suitcase, the GPU is the overhead bin, and the suitcase does not fit.

What is it 1:00 to 3:00

Quantization is saving the model as a JPEG instead of a RAW photo: much smaller, almost the same picture.

A model is billions of numbers. Its memory is how many numbers times how many bits each one takes. Quantization means storing each number with fewer bits. Fewer digits, same model.

Why should we care 3:00 to 6:00

If the model is smaller, it is cheaper to run, often faster, and it can run in places it could not before.

Cost: memory is often the reason you need an expensive GPU. Shrink the model, and a smaller, cheaper GPU can serve it. Speed: when a model writes text, each new word means reading its weights from memory. Fewer bytes to read usually means faster answers.

How it works 6:00 to 8:00

In my tests the 4-bit version used 62% less memory than the original and scored within a point on the exam.

Mechanism: take a group of numbers, find their range, and map each one to the nearest of a few levels. Keep one scale factor per group to map back. What you lose: fine distinctions between nearby values, and rare large values that do not fit the levels well.

Call to action 8:00 to 9:00

Before you buy a bigger GPU, benchmark a smaller number format.

Recap in one breath: fewer bits, much less memory, quality you can measure. The call to action: before you buy a bigger GPU, benchmark a smaller number format.
